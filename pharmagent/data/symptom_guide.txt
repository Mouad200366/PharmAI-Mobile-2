SYMPTOM GUIDE - Common Conditions and OTC Treatments
=====================================================

HEADACHE
- Mild to moderate headache: Acetaminophen 500mg every 4-6 hours (max 4g/day)
- Tension headache: Ibuprofen 200-400mg every 4-6 hours with food
- Headache with fever: Acetaminophen preferred over ibuprofen
- WARNING: Headache with stiff neck, confusion, or light sensitivity → seek emergency care immediately

FEVER
- Adults fever below 38.5C: Rest and fluids, no medication required
- Adults fever 38.5C-39.5C: Acetaminophen 500-1000mg every 6 hours OR Ibuprofen 400mg every 6 hours
- Fever above 39.5C persisting more than 3 days → consult a doctor
- Children fever: Always consult a doctor or pharmacist for appropriate pediatric dosing
- WARNING: Fever with rash, difficulty breathing, or confusion → emergency care immediately

SORE THROAT
- Mild sore throat: Warm salt water gargling, throat lozenges
- Sore throat with pain: Ibuprofen 200-400mg every 6 hours reduces inflammation
- Sore throat with fever above 38.5C lasting more than 2 days → may indicate bacterial infection, see doctor
- WARNING: Difficulty swallowing or breathing → emergency care immediately

COLD AND FLU
- Nasal congestion: Saline nasal spray, stay hydrated
- Runny nose and sneezing: Loratadine 10mg once daily (non-drowsy antihistamine)
- Body aches and fever: Ibuprofen 400mg every 6-8 hours with food
- Cough with mucus: Stay hydrated, honey in warm water
- WARNING: Flu symptoms in elderly, pregnant women, or immunocompromised → consult doctor promptly

ALLERGIES
- Seasonal allergies (sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes): Loratadine 10mg once daily
- Loratadine is non-drowsy and suitable for daytime use
- Mild skin allergy (rash, itching): Loratadine 10mg once daily
- WARNING: Severe allergic reaction (swelling of face/throat, difficulty breathing) → emergency care immediately, call for help

STOMACH PAIN / ACID REFLUX
- Heartburn and acid reflux: Omeprazole 20mg once daily before breakfast
- Omeprazole takes 1-4 days for full effect, not for immediate relief
- Stomach pain with nausea: Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin) as they worsen stomach issues
- WARNING: Severe stomach pain, vomiting blood, or black stools → emergency care immediately

MILD INFECTION / ANTIBIOTIC NOTE
- Antibiotics like Amoxicillin require a doctor prescription
- Never self-prescribe antibiotics
- Complete the full course if prescribed - never stop early
- WARNING: Signs of severe infection (high fever above 39C, spreading redness, swelling) → see doctor immediately

WHEN TO GO TO EMERGENCY - ALWAYS
- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Sudden severe headache
- Loss of consciousness or confusion
- Severe allergic reaction (face/throat swelling)
- Uncontrolled bleeding
- Suspected poisoning or overdose
