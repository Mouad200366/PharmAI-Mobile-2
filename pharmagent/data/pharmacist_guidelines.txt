PHARMACIST GUIDELINES - Safety Rules and Referral Criteria
===========================================================

DOSAGE SAFETY RULES
- Acetaminophen maximum: 4000mg per day for healthy adults, 2000mg for elderly or liver disease
- Ibuprofen maximum: 1200mg per day OTC (over the counter), up to 3200mg only under doctor supervision
- Aspirin: Not recommended for children under 16 (risk of Reye syndrome)
- Never recommend aspirin to patients with bleeding disorders or taking blood thinners
- Loratadine: 10mg once daily maximum, no dose adjustment needed for adults

PRESCRIPTION-ONLY MEDICINES (never recommend without prescription)
- Amoxicillin and all antibiotics
- Metformin (diabetes medication)
- Lisinopril (blood pressure medication)
- Atorvastatin (cholesterol medication)
- Salbutamol inhaler (asthma - requires diagnosis)
- Any medicine not explicitly listed as OTC

OTC MEDICINES (available without prescription)
- Acetaminophen / Paracetamol
- Ibuprofen (standard OTC doses)
- Aspirin (not for children under 16)
- Loratadine
- Omeprazole (low dose, short term)

DANGEROUS COMBINATIONS - NEVER RECOMMEND TOGETHER
- Ibuprofen + Aspirin: increased bleeding risk
- Ibuprofen + Lisinopril: reduces blood pressure medication effectiveness and kidney risk
- Aspirin + blood thinners (warfarin): severe bleeding risk
- Multiple NSAIDs together: never combine ibuprofen and aspirin for pain relief
- Acetaminophen + alcohol: severe liver damage risk

REFER TO DOCTOR IMMEDIATELY IF
- Patient has fever above 39.5C lasting more than 3 days
- Patient is pregnant and needs pain relief beyond simple paracetamol
- Patient is under 12 years old for most OTC medicines
- Patient has kidney disease and needs NSAIDs
- Patient has liver disease and needs acetaminophen
- Patient describes chest pain, difficulty breathing, or neurological symptoms
- Patient needs antibiotics (any suspected bacterial infection)
- Symptoms persist more than 7 days without improvement

EMERGENCY REFERRAL - CALL FOR HELP
- Any suspected overdose
- Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
- Chest pain with shortness of breath
- Loss of consciousness
